SAT MATH PRACTICE

Six questions with worked solutions

Try each question before opening the solution. The notes explain possible errors and how to avoid them. These are independent practice samples, not official College Board questions.

01 · ADVANCED MATH

Reconstructing a rational function

A function is defined by

f(x)=ax+bx−4,x≠4

where a and b are constants. Its graph passes through (0, −3) and (2, −5). The function can also be written as

f(x)=c+dx−4

where c and d are constants. If f(k) = 7, what is k + c + d?

Show solution

1. Find a and b.

Substitute the point (0, −3):

−3=b−4⇒b=12

Next, substitute the point (2, −5):

−5=2a+12−2⇒a=−1

2. Compare the two expressions.

Write the second expression as a single fraction:

c+dx−4=cx−4c+dx−4

Both expressions represent the same function, so

−x+12x−4=cx−4c+dx−4

Comparing coefficients of x gives c = −1. Comparing constant terms gives −4c + d = 12. Therefore, 4 + d = 12, so d = 8.

3. Find k and the requested sum.

−k+12k−4=7
−k+12=7k−28⇒k=5

Answer: k + c + d = 5 − 1 + 8 = 12.

Possible student mistakes
  • Expanding c(x − 4) as cx − 4, instead of cx − 4c.
  • Comparing d directly with 12 and overlooking −4c.
  • Finding k = 5 and stopping before calculating the requested sum.

02 · ADVANCED MATH

Finding a function from its composition

The function f is linear, and

f(f(x))=9x+8

for every real number x. If f(0) > 0, what is f(5)?

Show solution

1. Express f as a linear function.

Let f(x) = mx + b. Composing the function gives

f(f(x))=m(mx+b)+b
f(f(x))=m2x+b(m+1)

Compare coefficients with 9x + 8:

m2=9,b(m+1)=8

2. Use the condition f(0) > 0.

If m = 3, then 4b = 8, so b = 2.

If m = −3, then −2b = 8, so b = −4.

Since f(0) = b > 0, the valid function is f(x) = 3x + 2.

Answer: f(5) = 3(5) + 2 = 17.

Possible student mistakes
  • Treating 9x + 8 as f(x), rather than f(f(x)).
  • Taking only the positive solution of m² = 9 without checking the condition on f(0).
  • Assuming the constant term of the composition is the intercept of f.

03 · GEOMETRY AND ADVANCED MATH

Tangent lines to a circle

In the xy-plane, the line y = mx intersects the circle

(x−6)2+y2=9

at exactly one point. There are two possible values of m. What is their sum?

Show solution

1. Substitute the line into the circle.

Replace y with mx:

(x−6)2+(mx)2=9

Expand and collect like terms:

(1+m2)x2−12x+27=0

2. Set the discriminant equal to zero.

Exactly one intersection means the quadratic has exactly one real solution.

(−12)2−4(1+m2)(27)=0
36−108m2=0⇒m2=13

3. Add both possible slopes.

m=±13

Answer: The sum is 0.

Possible student mistakes
  • Expanding (mx)² as mx² instead of m²x².
  • Leaving the constant term as 36 instead of subtracting 9 to obtain 27.
  • Using a positive discriminant, which would give two intersections.
  • Finding only the positive value of m.

04 · PROBLEM SOLVING AND DATA ANALYSIS

Sampling and population estimates

A district has 18,000 students attending elementary, middle and high schools.

Study A: A simple random sample of 400 students from the entire district estimates that 46% travel by bus, with a margin of error of 5 percentage points.

Study B: A sample of 1,600 students drawn only from the district’s high schools finds that 44% travel by bus.

Which conclusion is best supported?

  1. Study A gives an estimated range of 7,380 to 9,180 bus riders across the district.
  2. Exactly 8,280 district students travel by bus.
  3. Study B gives a more reliable district-wide estimate solely because its sample is larger.
  4. Averaging the two percentages establishes that 45% of district students travel by bus.
Show solution

1. Apply the margin of error.

Study A’s interval extends 5 percentage points below and above 46%:

46−5=41,46+5=51

The estimated interval is 41% to 51%.

2. Convert the percentages to counts.

0.41×18000=7380
0.51×18000=9180

Study A therefore gives an estimated range of 7,380 to 9,180 students. This is an estimate, not a guaranteed count.

Answer: A.

Why the other choices are incorrect
  • B: 8,280 is the point estimate. It is not an established exact count.
  • C: A larger sample of high-school students does not address the exclusion of elementary and middle school students.
  • D: The studies sample different populations. A simple average does not establish the district-wide percentage.

05 · ALGEBRA

Revenue after a refund

A company sells 370 subscriptions. The number of premium subscriptions is 40 more than twice the number of basic subscriptions.

Basic subscriptions cost $25 each, and premium subscriptions cost $40 each.

The company issues a $5 refund on each premium subscription beyond the first 250. What is the total revenue after these refunds?

Show solution

1. Find the number of each subscription.

Let b be the number of basic subscriptions and p the number of premium subscriptions.

b+p=370,p=2b+40

Substitute the second equation into the first:

b+(2b+40)=370⇒b=110

Therefore, p = 370 − 110 = 260.

2. Calculate revenue before refunds.

25(110)+40(260)=13150

3. Subtract the refunds.

Only 260 − 250 = 10 premium subscriptions qualify. The total refund is $5 × 10 = $50.

Answer: $13,150 − $50 = $13,100.

Possible student mistakes
  • Reversing the relationship between basic and premium subscriptions.
  • Reporting $13,150 and forgetting to subtract the refunds.
  • Refunding all 260 premium subscriptions instead of only the 10 beyond the first 250.

06 · ADVANCED MATH

Interpreting an exponential growth rate

The number of bacteria in a culture is modeled by

N(t)=A(1.69)t3

where A is a positive constant and t is measured in hours.

According to the model, the population increases by r% during each 1.5-hour interval. What is the value of r?

Show solution

1. Identify the current time interval.

The model has a growth factor of 1.69 every 3 hours. This represents a 69% increase during each 3-hour interval.

N(t) =A (1.69) t3

2. Express the model in 1.5-hour intervals.

We want 1.5 in the denominator of the exponent. Since 3 = 2 × 1.5, write

N(t) =A (1.69) t 2×1.5

Separate the factor of one-half from the exponent:

N(t) =A [ (1.69) 12 ] t1.5

Since the square root of 1.69 is 1.3, the model becomes

N(t) =A (1.3) t1.5

3. Read the new growth rate.

The denominator is now 1.5, so the population is multiplied by 1.3 every 1.5 hours. This represents a 30% increase.

r= (1.3−1) ×100 =30

Answer: 30.

Remember: In the form below, the quantity is multiplied by b every h time units.

A b th
Possible student mistakes
  • Dividing 69% by 2 to obtain 34.5%, instead of accounting for compound growth.
  • Reporting 69%, which is the increase over three hours.
  • Reporting 130%, confusing the percentage of the previous population with the percentage increase.

DIFFICULTY PHILOSOPHY

The mathematics should remain SAT mathematics. The challenge should come from what the student has to notice.

NOT HARD

Ugly numbers

Long arithmetic does not automatically create meaningful difficulty.

NOT HARD

Unnecessary steps

A problem can be tedious without requiring stronger reasoning.

USEFUL HARD

Connected ideas

Difficulty becomes useful when familiar concepts must be recognized and combined in an unfamiliar arrangement.
